The game in three parts

You do not get to pick your life. You get to live it.

01

A city with eight floors

Chongqing is built vertically. Its districts run from semi-basement rooms carved out of air-raid shelters, through riverside stilt houses and flats under a bridge approach, up through the old stair-terrace neighbourhoods, the 1990s blocks with lifts, the high-rise towers and finally the rooftops.

Eight layers, L0 to L7, each with its own housing forms, its own rent and its own way of getting through a day.

8 vertical layers · 37 districts
02

Which floor you live on decides everything

Your birthplace is drawn for you, weighted by real birth counts across Chongqing's 37 districts. What you choose is the floor.

That single choice rewrites your commute, your rent, your stamina, your health, your relationships and the ending you get. Live at L0 and you are in a bunker room with no window. Live at L7 and the whole river is yours, in an oven in August.

The floor is the build
03

A life you cannot reset

You are born, you work, and a handful of times something is genuinely yours to decide — whether to follow your parents when they move, whether to take the job, whether to keep carrying it when your body asks you to stop. After each one, you live with it, all the way to the end.

When it ends you get a life card and a link. Anyone who opens that link sees the exact life you lived.

The eight layers

Eight ways to live in one city

Every layer is a different set of buildings, a different rent, a different commute and a different body. Read a layer before you pick it.

  • L0UndergroundBunkers and converted parking bays. No air in summer, no sun at all.
  • L1River LevelRiverside stilt houses. River wind and ship horns; the ground floor takes water in flood season.
  • L2Bridge LevelFlats under a bridge approach. Cars overhead all night, but the rent holds down.
  • L3Street LevelShops on the ground, homes above. You can buy cigarettes without going out.
  • L4Stair LevelThe old neighbourhoods. Even the ground floor is fifty steps above the street.
  • L5ResidentialThe standard lifted block. Nothing special, nothing wrong. The safe bet.
  • L6TowerHigh-rise floors. You can see the river and the bridges, and the wind will not let you keep the window open.
  • L7RooftopThe top floor. An oven in summer, a wind tunnel in winter — and the whole river is yours alone.

Each layer has its own page with the housing forms, the rent and the daily reality of living there — for example L4 · Stair Level, L0 · Underground and L7 · Rooftop.

What actually happens

A whole life, played out, and then closed

You are born somewhere you did not choose. The birthplace is drawn from real birth data across Chongqing's 37 districts. There is no menu and no reroll — which is the first thing the game is trying to teach you.

You will be a child in someone else's city, and you will find out what that feels like before the game explains anything.

You work, and you get tired. Stamina, rent, health and money move year by year. The floor sets the terms: a walk-up block is different from a tower with a lift, and both are different from a fourth-floor room above a shop.

Six people orbit you — a neighbour, a colleague, a relative, the landlord, a child, and the old man opposite. They are not numbers with names; they are people who show up.

Seven times, something is genuinely yours. Each of those moments has several real answers and every one of them costs you something different. Choose, then live with it for the rest of the run.

At the end you get a life card: a vertical cross-section of Chongqing with your own trajectory drawn across it, and a link that opens your exact life for anyone who clicks it.

Questions

Frequently asked

Is the game free?

Yes, and it stays free. There is no account, no subscription, no advertisement, no timer and no paywall. Open the link in any modern browser and play. It works on a phone and it works on a laptop.

Why are you asking for $9 if the game is free?

The $9 is not for access to the game. It is for a commemorative card — a physical or digital keepsake printed with the details of the life you actually lived: your year, your district, your layer, your floor, and a unique serial number. If you never buy it, nothing about the game changes and you lose nothing at all.

What exactly is the commemorative card?

It is a card with the same information the game already shows you at the end of a life, laid out as a print. The digital version is generated in the browser after payment, with your serial number, and you can download or share it as an image. The physical version is the same design, printed and posted to an address you give us. We use that address for nothing else.

Does paying unlock anything?

No. There is nothing to unlock. Every layer, every ending and every choice is already available to every player for free, and paying cannot buy a better outcome, a different birthplace or a different ending.

Can I really not choose where I am born?

Correct. The birthplace is drawn for you, weighted by real birth counts across Chongqing's 37 districts. You choose the floor. That is the only build choice, and it is a large one.

Can I replay or reset a life?

Not your own — the whole point is that a life happens once, from birth to death. What you can do is send the link you get at the end to someone else. They will open the exact same life: same seed, same choices, same ending. That is the only way to "reroll" it, and it is somebody else's job.

Which languages is it in?

English, 中文, 日本語, 한국어 and Español. You can switch with the language menu at the top of the game, or open the game directly in a given language using the links at the top of this page.

Can I stream or record it and make money from it?

Yes. You are free to stream, record and publish gameplay, including on monetised channels, with no permission form and no revenue share. The only two asks are that a link to the game appears in your description and that you call it a life simulator. Assets are in the free creator pack.

Is this based on a real place?

The city is real: the districts, the eight vertical layers and the housing forms are drawn from how Chongqing is actually built. The simulation is a model built on real statistics, not a claim of historical accuracy. It is a life simulator, not a documentary.
